## Primary research consulted

- Piri et al., *3D-printed fuel-cell bipolar plates for evaluating flow-field performance*, Clean Energy 4(2), 142–157 (2020), DOI [10.1093/ce/zkaa007](https://academic.oup.com/ce/article/4/2/142/5854478). The authors fabricated flow-field prototypes using PolyJet/SLA and evaluated pressure drop and velocity distributions. This supports depicting printed fluid-path geometry as a research tool, rather than only a mechanical support. The publisher's indexed abstract was accessible; direct full-text opening failed in the research tool.
- Niblett, Elwan and Mamlouk, *Membraneless water electrolysis enabled by flow and porous electrode design for bubble separation*, Chemical Engineering Journal 519, 163444 (2025), DOI [10.1016/j.cej.2025.163444](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cej.2025.163444). The authors used transparent SLA-printed laboratory cells to investigate flow and bubble transport. This is evidence for custom research-cell bodies and observation of fluid behavior, not blanket chemical compatibility of any printable resin. Publisher-indexed abstract and fabrication excerpt were accessible.
- *3D-Printed electrodes for membraneless water electrolysis*, Sustainable Energy & Fuels (2020), DOI [10.1039/C9SE00710E](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2020/se/c9se00710e). The reported porous electrodes used conductive-composite printing followed by nickel deposition; the gas manifold used resin printing. This is a separate functional-material/post-processing workflow, not evidence that ordinary printed plastic is a working electrocatalyst. Publisher-indexed abstract and fabrication excerpt were accessible; direct full-text opening returned 403.
- Sun et al., *3D printing of interdigitated Li-ion microbattery architectures*, Advanced Materials 25(33), 4539–4543 (2013), DOI [10.1002/adma.201301036](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23776158/). The abstract describes printing lithium-oxide-based inks into interdigitated anode/cathode structures. This requires specialized electrode inks and cell fabrication, and must not be presented as an established JAFORD printing service. PubMed abstract opened successfully.
